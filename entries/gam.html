Fan Wan entry
(Not present)
Williams (1856) entry
  • (Page 173 #9: ꜂噉)
    • An adverb of quality, so, thus, this manner;
    • an interjection of surprise;
    • ꜂kòm ꜃yéung yéung꜅ tsò꜅ tak꜆ (gam2 joeng6-2 zou6 dak1 噉樣做得) this will do, very well;
    • tsau꜅ ꜂kòm (zau6 gam2 就噉), stop, well;
    • ꜂kòm ꜃yéung yéung꜅ ꜂tsz' (gam2 joeng6 zi2 噉樣子) such a style! used in depreciation;
    • ꜂kòm wá꜅ (gam2 waa6 噉話) if you say so, if it be so;
    • ꜀tʻung ꜀tò hai꜅ ꜂kòm ké꜄ (tung1 dou1 hai6 gam2 ge3 通都係噉嘅) they are all just so, all alike;
    • ꜂kòm ké꜄! ꜁'m ꜂hò ꜂kòm (gam2 ge3-2! m4 hou2 gam2 噉嘅、唔好噉), so! don't do so, that'll not do;
    • ꜂kòm ꜂tám tsò꜅ (gam2 daam2 zou6 噉膽做) so daring, brave.
Conway's commentary
  • Even in modern Cantonese, this word is conservatively pronounced [kom], not [kɐm]. See below.
  • Williams' 噉樣做得: Corrected ꜃yéung (joeng5) to yéung꜅ (joeng6-2).
  • Williams' 噉樣子: Corrected ꜃yéung (joeng5) to yéung꜅ (joeng6). A more modern equivalent would be 噉嘅樣 gam2 ge3 joeng6-2.
  • Williams' 噉嘅: This is a rhetorical interrogative, better rendered "like so?" than "so!".
  • Williams' 噉膽做: Better rendered "[to] do [with] gall like so". Not the best example with which to demonstrate 噉.
Conway's Cantonese entry
  • 【噉】 (gam2)
    1. Like so, like that, in such a manner, this way.
      • 乜你噉㗎 (mat1 nei5 gam2 gaa3): "Why [are] you like this?"
      • 噉樣 (gam2 joeng6-2): "Like so."
      • 噉嘅樣 (gam2 ge3 joeng6-2): "Looking like that."
      • 爛成噉 (laan6 seng4 gam2): "Broken to such [a degree]."
      • 就噉 (zau6 gam2): "Leave it at that."
      • 噉點得㗎 (gam2 dim2 dak1 gaa3): "How [is] this acceptable?"
      • 噉至得𠺢嘛 (gam2 zi3 dak1 ga1 maa3): "Only like so [will it be] acceptable."
      • 你噉都唔知 (nei5 gam2 dou1 m4 zi1): "[How can] you not even know [a simple thing] like that?"
      • 成個乞兒噉 (seng4 go3 hat1 ji4-1 gam2): "Like [a] complete beggar."
      • 如果你好似狗一樣噉食嘢 (jyu4 gwo2 nei5 hou2 ci5 gau2 jat1 joeng6 gam2 sik6 je5): "If you [will] eat things in such [a] manner [that is the] same as [a] dog" (see Kill Bill: Volume 2).
    2. Used conjunctively.
      • 噉走㗎喇 (gam2 zau2 ga3 laa3): "So [then we'll be] leaving."
  • 【係噉意】 (hai6 gam2 ji3-2)
    1. With barely any effort put in. (Literally, "intention being like so".)
      • 係噉意 (hai6 gam2 ji3-2): "[I] barely made [an] effort" (used when presenting someone with a gift).
      • 係噉意揩吓 (hai6 gam2 ji3-2 haai1 haa5): "Brushed against with barely any effort" (i.e. not washed properly).
  • 【係噉先】 (hai6 gam2 sin1)
    1. Used to wrap up a conversation. (Literally, "[let it] be like so [at] first".)
  • 【就噉】 (zau6 gam2)
    1. To leave it at that, to not do any more.

The spelling 噉 for gam2 is arbitrary (but well established). The alternative spelling 咁 is also very common.

The word 噉 gam2 ("like so") is conservatively pronounced [kom], not [kɐm]. (Arguably, it would be better to romanise the conservative pronunciation as gum2 rather than gam2, but standard Jyutping does not permit this.)

Alongside 咁 gam3 ("so/that"), 噉 gam2 ("like so") is one of the few words with a Williams final of òm that has resisted the shift to am in modern Cantonese.

Note that 噉 gam2 ("like so") is a distinct word from 錦 gam2 (particle of partiality). The latter cannot be pronounced [kom].

Fan Wan entry
  • (Vol. 2 Image 37 錦) 五色絲所織
Williams (1856) entry
  • (Page 125 #3: ꜂錦)
    • A kind Ornamental work of variegated silk used in ornamental work (Kangxi: 襄色織文也 etc.);
    • embroidered, worked, or knit in colors;
    • elegant, figurative fine writing;
    • flowery, pictured;
    • ꜀í í꜄ ꜂kam (ji3 gam2 衣錦), the gentry, official persons;
    • ꜂kam ꜁chʻau (gam2 cau4 錦綢), figured pongee;
    • shap꜇ ꜂kam ꜂ún típ꜇ (sap6 gam2 wun2 dip6 什錦碗碟) colored chinaware;
    • ꜂kam sau꜄ ꜁man (gam2 sau3 man4 錦繡文), an elegant style.
Conway's commentary
  • Williams' "A kind of variegated silk used in ornamental work": This is a poor rendering of both Kangxi 襄色織文也, which defines 錦 as the ornamental work (not the silk), and Fan Wan 五色絲所織, which defines 錦 as 所織 "that weaved" (not the silk).
  • Williams' 衣錦: Corrected ꜀í (ji1) to í꜄ (ji3).
Conway's literary rendering
  • 【錦】 (kimX)
    • (noun-like) brocade
Conway's Cantonese entry
  • 【錦】 (gam2)
    1. A particle of partial amount.
      • 食錦啲先 (sik6 gam2 di1 sin1): "Eat [a] bit of [it] first."
      • 冇錦唿 (mou5 gam2 fat1): "Lost [a] patch of [it]."

The spelling 錦 is arbitrary. The alternative spelling 𡄎 has poorer font support.

The spellings 噉 and 咁 are inadvisable, because 噉 gam2 ("like so") is a distinct word. Unlike 噉 gam2 ("like so"), the particle 錦 gam2 cannot be pronounced [kom].

咁 Kòm꜄ (gam3)

Radical + residual strokes
⼝ + 5
Unicode code point
U+5481
Fan Wan entry
(Not present)
Williams (1856) entry
  • (Page 173 #10: 咁꜄)
    • An adverb of quantity;
    • so, such;
    • kòm꜄ ꜃ün (gam3 jyun5 咁遠), so far!
    • kòm꜄ ꜂tsò (gam3 zou2 咁早), so early!
    • kòm꜄ mat꜆ ꜃yé (gam3 mat1 je5 咁乜嘢), why so? what is the reason?
    • séung꜄ mau꜅ máu꜅ kòm꜄ ꜂chʻau (soeng3 maau6 gam3 cau2 相貌咁醜), such an ugly face;
    • kòm꜄ ꜀shá ꜂tám (gam3 saa1 daam2 咁沙膽), such great courage;
    • kòm꜄ ꜀to (gam3 do1 咁多), so many.
Conway's commentary
  • Even in modern Cantonese, this word is conservatively pronounced [kom], not [kɐm]. See below.
  • Williams' 相貌咁醜: Corrected mau꜅ (mau6) to máu꜅ (maau6).
Conway's Cantonese entry
  • 【咁】 (gam3)
    1. So, that, to such an extent.
      • 乜你咁蠢㗎 (mat1 nei5 gam3 ceon2 gaa3): "Why [are] you so stupid?"
      • 得咁多 (dak1 gam3 do1): "[There's] only that much."
      • 咁晏 (gam3 aan3): "That late [in the day]?"
      • 咁夜 (gam3 je6): "That late [at night]?"
      • 咁誇張 (gam3 kwaa1 zoeng1): "[Is it] that ridiculous?"
      • 咁得人驚 (gam3 dak1 jan4 geng1): "[Is it] that frightful?"
      • 冇你咁好氣 (mou5 nei5 gam3 hou2 hei3): "[I] don't have that good endurance as you" (i.e. I won't persist with you).
  • 【咁上下】 (gam3 soeng6 haa6-2)
    1. Roughly that much, roughly to that extent.

The spelling 咁 is arbitrary (but well established).

The word 咁 gam3 ("so/that") is conservatively pronounced [kom], not [kɐm]. (Arguably, it would be better to romanise the conservative pronunciation as gum3 rather than gam3, but standard Jyutping does not permit this.)

Alongside 噉 gam2 ("like so"), 咁 gam3 ("so/that") is one of the few words with a Williams final of òm that has resisted the shift to am in modern Cantonese.
