Williams (1856) entry
  • (Page 211 #11: 纊꜄ Kwáng꜄ (gwaang3); here normalised to Kwʻang꜄ (kwang3))

    A colloquial word;

    (…)

    • To rub against, to run upon one in the street;
    • kwáng꜄ kwʻang꜄ ꜀tsʻan ꜃kʻü (kwang3 can1 keoi5 纊嚫佢), rubbed against it;
    • kwáng꜄ kwʻang꜄ lán꜅ ꜀shám (kwang3 laan6 saam1 纊爛衫), to tear one's dress by catching it;
    • kwáng꜄ kwʻang꜄ ꜃kʻü lok꜇ ꜁lai (kwang3 keoi5 lok6 lai4 纊佢落嚟), catch it (a kite) down;
    • ꜂tʻai kwáng꜄ kwʻang꜄ (tai2 kwang3 睇纊) lookout, [lest I] run against you!—a porter's cry.
Conway's Cantonese entry
  • 【纊】 (kwang3)
    1. To graze or catch against, especially injuriously. More forceful than 揩 haai1 "brush against", but less severe than 撞 zong6 "crash".
      • 畀樹枝纊嚫 (bei2 syu6 zi1 kwang3 can1): "Caught against by [a] twig."

The spelling 纊 is arbitrary (but well established).
